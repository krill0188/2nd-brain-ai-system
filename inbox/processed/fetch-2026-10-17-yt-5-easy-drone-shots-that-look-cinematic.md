So, grab your controller and let's take your drone shots to the next level. First up is flying in a straight path. This technique is extremely simple, but can be very effective. Depending on your scene, flying directly straight or sideways can be a great way to introduce a scene or just have a peaceful clip to showcase the area. For this shot, all you're going to do is find a path where it's safe to fly straight and slowly push the right stick up allowing the drone to fly in a straight line. Again, this is a very simple shot, but it can lead to some cool results. The second shot is flying with your camera pointed straight down. This is similar to the first shot, but just from a different perspective. First, you'll rotate your gimbal all the way down to -90°. In my case, I do that by using a dial on the back of my controller. From here, you do the same movement as number one, which is pushing the right stick up and flying straight. This technique can be a cool perspective that you can only achieve with a drone. The third shot is orbiting a subject. To do this, you will pick a direction you want to rotate and push the right stick slowly in that direction. As you're doing that, you'll want to press the left stick in the opposite direction. This will give an orbiting effect. Orbiting shots can be great to showcase any kind of subject and make it look dramatic. I almost always use orbits in any shoot I do. Just remember to go slowly and especially push the stick slowly. This will ensure a cinematic and smooth feel. The fourth technique is called the dronie. You start close up on a person and then slowly fly back and up until you reveal the entire location around that person. This one is great if you want to reveal a beautiful location and also give it some perspective at the same time. A trick with these is to speed the middle part up in editing, so you can get to the big reveal shot faster if that fits your edit. The fifth and final technique I'll share with you today is tilting the camera up or down while flying straight. To pull this shot off, you will fly straight with your camera pointed down, usually around 90°. As you continue to fly straight, slowly start to turn that gimbal dial so the gimbal starts to rotate upward. Keep doing this until the gimbal is flat or at 0°. A little trick is when approaching 0°, start to press the dial even softer. If you don't, the gimbal will abruptly stop at 0, and the shot will look a little clunky. This is great for any type of shot where you want to reveal something, but also showcase the area around. All of these do require some practice, especially the last three, but just give it time. I can guarantee that there will be times you will mess up, accidentally go too fast, too slow, or just hit the wrong stick. Don't worry, we've all been there before. It takes time to build up muscle memory for which stick controls what, especially when you have to control both sticks and adjust the dial at the same time.
